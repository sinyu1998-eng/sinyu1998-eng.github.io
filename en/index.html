HARDWARE · AI GLASSES · FACTORY EDITION

Same hardware,
redefined asthe factory's eyes

The AI GLASSES (AiMB-G3) I have here started as a consumer product: 8MP camera, WiFi, 1200p recording, AI recognition, multilingual translation. I reconfigured it as an inspection and patrol device — wired into SellerPilot work orders so every inspection leaves first-person, traceable evidence.

Tap a point on the frame to inspect a part

Incoming inspection · IQC

QC puts on the glasses and walks into the raw-material area.

Camera on incoming goods → AI reads SKU and batch labels → quantities and specs are checked against the SellerPilot work order → discrepancies (wrong item, damage, colour drift) flag up on the spot, with a photo logged into the inspection record. An 8MP still is plenty for label and appearance checks.

01

Hardware hardening

Dust- and oil-resistant lenses, a temple strap (stays on when bending or lifting), spare batteries and a charging case. Three lens sets cover bright warehouses, indoor lines and near-sighted workers — the consumer version already had them, so we reuse them.

02

Firmware and device management

Flashed as a managed enterprise device: personal accounts and social sharing disabled, self-check on boot, recordings forced into SellerPilot work orders, with a time + work-order watermark burned into the frame. Factories don't want cool, they want tamper-proof.

03

Work orders and the evidence chain

Anything the glasses capture is attached to the order or batch, producing an inspection-report PDF plus timestamped video that clients, platforms, insurers and buyers can re-check. This is the part factories will actually pay for.

Cost basis: consumer retail $199 → factory-edition target BOM ¥1,200–1,500 per unit, pilots from 10 units, one line and one product category first. The full plan (hardware/software inventory, three rollout routes, 90-day schedule) is in the Glasses × SellerPilot roadmap →。
